Decision — the submit bar stays after submitting

Filing the decision currently replaces the whole card body, and the submit bar goes with it: the one control the reviewer just pressed vanishes from under their pointer. This keeps the bar on screen in a fourth state — filed, non-interactive, in the same box — so the row the hand is resting on is still there when the record appears above it.

1 · The bar's four states, in order

Three exist today. The fourth is the proposal, and it is a state of the same component rather than a replacement for it — same 36px left slot, same flexed sentence, same two-line button with a 16px glyph slot held open at all times. Nothing on this row changes width or height between the third state and the fourth.

The glyph slot is what makes this free. SubmitBar already mounts the spinner unconditionally and hides it with invisible, precisely so that pressing Submit does not widen the button by 26px. The tick lands in that same slot, and the rim is an inset ring rather than a border — so state 3 → state 4 is a fill change and a glyph swap on a box whose width and height do not move by a pixel.

2 · Where it sits in the filed card

Last child of the card, after What changed — the same position it holds in the live card, which is the point. The stamp at the top of the record and the bar at the bottom both say the decision is filed, and they are ~600px apart in a card you scroll: the stamp heads the record, the bar is the resting state of the action. They are not competing for one glance.

No second Reopen. The record's stamp already carries it, and the way back from a filed decision should have exactly one door. The bar's left slot holds its 36px and paints nothing, so the sentence keeps its x — putting Reopen there instead is the one real alternative, and §4 of the plan says why not yet.

3 · Why a wash and not a fill

The obvious move is the button's own success variant — bg-success text-white. It is the wrong one twice over, and the second reason is measured.

rejected — solid success, white ink

A filled block at full strength reads as the primary action of the card — pressable, and the next thing to do. It is neither.

chosen — the folder's TONE.ok

A rim and a wash of the success hue, with neutral foreground ink. Reads as a receipt: coloured enough to be read as a pass, quiet enough not to be pressed.

And the contrast settles it. White on --success measures 4.25:1 — under the 4.5 an AA pass wants for 13px semibold, which is exactly the figure FiledRecord.tsx already records when it rejected solid fills for its change badges. foreground on a /10 wash measures 13.2–14.7:1 and passes in both themes. Reusing TONE.ok means this button is not a fourth opinion about the same question.

4 · Dark

--success is re-declared in .dark at a lighter chroma, so a color-mix against it — which is what bg-success/10 and border-success/30 compile to — tracks the theme on its own. No dark-only override, and no hard-coded green anywhere in the state.

5 · The sentence, frozen

The paragraph beside the button is computed from LIVE state today (departuresOf(state, limit)). After filing it has to be computed from the snapshot instead, for the same reason FiledRecord takes a frozen evidence list: a record that re-derives itself from a store that can still move is not a record. Its three readings gain a fourth, in the same voice.

all four readings of one slot

unselected   No resolution selected.

armed, agreed   No change in position.

armed, departed   Change in position · 2 of 4 parts: resolution and refund.

filed   Filed · change in position, 2 of 4 parts: resolution and refund.

filed, agreed   Filed · no change in position.

Past tense, not a new fact. Filed takes the bold lead the other three readings give their first clause, and the departure count follows it lowercased — so the row still answers "what went, and did anybody move it" without the stamp above having to be read first. No timestamp here: the stamp prints one 600px up, and two would be two places to disagree.
