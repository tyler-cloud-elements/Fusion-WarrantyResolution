Decision — opening with nothing selected

The card no longer opens holding a decision nobody made. The recommendation is still stated twice on purpose: the head says what the agent proposes, the card it belongs to wears the badge. Submit stays dim until a person picks one.

One wrapping row in every state, so the head cannot change height on the first click and reflow the card under the pointer. The confidence stays at full strength while nothing is selected — it is the agent's confidence in its own proposal, and nothing has contradicted it yet.

The alternative was two stacked rows — "No resolution selected" on its own line with the recommendation beneath it. It reads a little clearer in isolation and costs the card a line that disappears on the first click, which is the reflow this row avoids. Say the word if you would rather have the clarity.

2 · The resolution cards — now, and two ways forward

Today all three sit on bg-card, the same surface as the panel holding them, so they read as regions rather than controls. Both options fill the unselected state with bg-muted; they differ in how the selected one is drawn.

now — card ground, border-only selection, Deny preselected, no badge Resolution
option A — muted ground, tinted selection · recommended · initial state, nothing selected Resolution
option A — Deny picked · the badge stays where the agent put it Resolution
option A — Partial picked · the badge is now the only thing saying what the agent wanted Resolution
option B — muted ground, solid selection, matching Submit · Partial picked Resolution
option B — Deny picked, so the badge lands on teal and has to change colour Resolution

Why A. B is the literal reading of "in line with the submit button", and the two cards above show what it costs: the radio marker, the description and the badge all have to invert on the one card that is selected, the insight tint has to leave the badge — which is the hue this app uses to mean "agent" everywhere — and a solid teal card sits 200px above a solid teal Submit, so the choice competes with the control that files it. A gives you the filled ground and a clearly-pressed state without any of that.

3 · The submit bar

The sentence is a two-way switch today. With nothing selected it would say "No change in position." beside a dead button, which reads as a broken control rather than a step not yet taken — so it gets a third value in the same slot and the same voice.

And a bug this would otherwise ship. departuresOf computes s.resolution !== s.agent.outcome, so "" !== "Denied" makes the bar read "Change in position · 1 of 4 parts: resolution." at mount, before anybody has done anything. The plan's §5 guards it.
